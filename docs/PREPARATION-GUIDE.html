Study System

How I'm preparing for the CCA exam

A practical guide to building your own study system — from calendar scheduling to voice transcripts to auto-generated quizzes. Everything shown here was built by prompting an agent. You can do the same.

The Pipeline
1
Set the container

Schedule study blocks in Google Calendar

Decision fatigue kills consistency. Before touching any study material, block time in your calendar. Weekdays: 1 hour reading + 1 hour testing. Weekends: 2 hours reading + 1 hour review + 2 hours testing. Once it's on the calendar, showing up is automatic.

You can do this manually in the Google Calendar UI, or have your agent do it through the CLI. Either way, the important thing is that every day has a specific module assigned — not just "study CCA."

Prompt: "Create recurring Google Calendar events for my CCA study. Weekdays 8-10 PM, weekends 9-11 AM and 8-10 PM. Map each session to a specific domain from the CCA exam guide. Use gcalcli to create the events."

2
Prime the brain

Record a freestyle walkthrough as you read

At the start of each new module, open your voice recorder and talk through what you're reading. This isn't a polished presentation — it's a brain dump. Say what makes sense, what confuses you, what you think the trade-offs are, what seems like it would break in production.

The act of verbalizing forces you to find the gaps. If you can't explain something out loud, you don't understand it yet. The recording becomes raw material for the next step.

Prompt: "I just recorded a 20-minute walkthrough of [topic]. Here's the transcript. Read through it and identify: (1) concepts I clearly understand, (2) concepts I'm fuzzy on, (3) things I got wrong, and (4) questions I raised but didn't answer. For each gap, give me a clear explanation."

3
Build the brain

Agent builds your Obsidian vault from the transcript

The agent reads your transcript, fills in your gaps, and creates structured notes in Obsidian. This is where passive reading becomes an active, linked knowledge base. Every concept gets a note. Every note links to other notes. Every claim links to a source.

Daily Notes

One note per session: focus, sources read, quiz score, weak areas, and auto-generated flashcards. One keystroke opens today's note.

Templates

Define a daily-note skeleton once. Agent inserts it with one command. Consistent structure across every session.

Tags

Tag every note with #cca/domain1 through #cca/domain5. Click a tag to see everything for that domain. Graph View colors by tag.

Backlinks

Open any note and see every other note that references it. Write an anti-pattern once, see it linked from every session where you got burned by it.

Dataview

Plugin that treats your vault like a database. Query: "show all flashcards tagged #cca/retest sorted by date." No manual tracking.

Graph View

The visual network. Every wiki-link becomes a node. Domains cluster naturally. Gap areas show as isolated nodes. The most-linked concepts are visually central.

Obsidian Graph View — what your vault looks like

Every node is a note. Every line is a wiki-link. The dense cluster on the left is a well-studied domain — notes naturally interlink. The isolated node on the right with the dashed border and alert badge marks a domain you haven't touched yet. Gaps are impossible to miss.

4
Test the knowledge

Generate HTML quizzes that track your progress

Passive review doesn't work. You need to make decisions under time pressure. Have your agent build a self-contained HTML quiz file for each domain. Open in any browser — no internet, no server, no dependencies.

What the quiz does

Each attempt draws 20 random questions from a 30-question bank. Options are shuffled so you can't memorize positions. Explanations are shuffle-proof — they never reference option letters.

A pass requires 1000/1000. Two perfect passes mark the domain complete. Wrong answers accumulate in localStorage and appear as "Weak Areas" grouped by topic on the results screen.

Keyboard controls: 1-4 or A-D to select, Enter to advance, arrow keys to navigate. 15-minute timer with warning at 2 minutes.

Prompt to generate a quiz from scratch: "Build me a self-contained HTML quiz for CCA Domain [N]. 30 scenario-based questions with 4 plausible options each. Requirements: (1) 20 questions per attempt, randomly drawn and shuffled, (2) localStorage pass tracking — 2 perfect passes needed, (3) localStorage wrong-answer accumulation with per-topic grouping in results, (4) shuffle-proof explanations — never reference option letters, (5) keyboard controls (1-4/A-D/Enter/arrows), (6) 15-minute timer, (7) dark theme with [color] accent, (8) zero dependencies, works from file://. Read the Anthropic docs for this domain before generating questions."
Prompt to iterate on an existing quiz: "Question [N] has a distractor that's too obviously wrong — rewrite it to be more plausible. Question [M]'s correct answer is consistently the longest — rebalance all 4 options. Question [K]'s explanation references 'option B' which breaks when shuffled — make it shuffle-proof."
The Daily Loop

How one session flows

Calendar notification arrives. You open the doc, hit record on your voice memos, and talk through what you're reading for 15-20 minutes. You paste the transcript to your agent. It identifies your gaps, answers your questions, creates Obsidian notes, and refreshes the quiz for that domain. You take the quiz. If you miss anything, you retake it immediately — same session. The cycle repeats when the next calendar block fires.
